Tin Bird, as the essays' host

An ideation sketch, made on 24 Nov 2026, with no code landed. "Teen Bird" is Tin Bird (cast.ts tin-owlet): the transcription heard "tin" as "teen". It is the bird already on main (packages/some-styles/src/brand/cast.ts, the tutor and the thumbnails' bird). Here it gets lifelike loops and a fixed set of postures and expressions a scene can call by name, rendered through the pipeline main already has (@some-ui/video-motion's HyperFrames compositions, then motion video).

Recommendation: A, the SVG rig, landing as a burn-in kind.Poses, expressions and placements combine as parameters instead of as a grid of pictures. It stays sharp at close-up size, and the same inputs gave the same bytes. B, the sprite sheet, is just as deterministic, but its size grows with every pose, expression and direction multiplied together, and it blurs in a close-up.

The bird on this page moves live in your browser, by the same rig code the renders use. The videos below are the renders.

The sample essay, twice: A and B

Template sheet: every pose by every expression, moving

Rows are postures and columns are expressions; any pair combines. In the look and point rows the owlet follows the small target as it circles. Idle life runs underneath everything: a blink on a seeded schedule (now and then a double), glances, a breath and a slow sway. The wind-up key turns, faster while it speaks, and the body gives a small shake as it talks: both are what you asked for in C823 ("can the tin bird's wind-up key spin while it's speaking… shake a little"), drawn here as members of the same rig. All of it is a pure function of time and a seed.

Where B would win: a painterly character that code can't draw (watercolour, hand-inked frames). The owlet is already vector data in the cast, so the sprite only copies it at lower fidelity. A sprite sheet stays a sound way to export the host for somewhere that can't run the rig (a GIF, a sticker), made from A on demand.

The family it joins: a burn-in kind, beside captions and cards

I looked inward first. A burn-in (packages/ui/burn-ins/src/lib/burn-in.ts) is already a closed union of kinds, each with a frozen design, params checked once, and a pure frameAt(t). The recorder seeks every frame and lays an alpha layer over any picture: a capture, a snip, a card or a composition. That is the host: a layer that lives over the scene, timed in milliseconds and pure in time. So I'd add a host kind with a design tin-owlet-v1, not a new field on the shot. Its beats are a closed union the compiler checks: pose × expression, with a placement over the scene.

__TS__

Your choice: the corner. The burn-ins' regions never overlap (src/lib/geometry.ts), and none of them is a corner, so a corner host either carves its own region out of the body's bottom corner (cards then lay out around it, a little narrower in every scene), or the host is declared an exception that may draw over the body while the cards stay as they are (simpler, though the host can then cover a card's last line). A close-up host is a full-frame layer either way, so other burn-ins step aside while it holds.

Next week, and what's open

  • The wings are new. The cast's still owlet has no wings, and pointing needed them, so the sketch adds two riveted tin wings and also gives the key a bow that turns. That's a change to the brand's drawing: cast.ts would carry the wings as parts the still pose tucks away.
  • Which bird hosts. I used Tin Bird (tin-owlet): it is the tutor, the essays are pedagogical, and the thumbnails already draw it as the synthetic voice. tin-sparrow, the cast's other tin bird (the asker), could co-host on the same rig. The barn owl you set aside in C823 is not drawn on anywhere here.
  • Voice. With home's speaker, the beak would open per word from the take's word timings (C821 step 2), where it now opens on a fixed syllable rhythm.
  • Render time. HyperFrames draws about 0.6 s a frame here. As a burn-in, the host would be recorded once as an alpha layer, as captions are.
